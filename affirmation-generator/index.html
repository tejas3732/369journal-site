      How to use your affirmation

      1. Pick one. Choose the affirmation that feels most believable when you read it out loud.
      2. Keep the words the same every time you write it.
      3. Use the 369 method: write it 3 times in the morning, 6 in the afternoon and 9 at night. See how to do the 369 method.
      4. Take one small step a day towards what you wrote.

      What makes a good affirmation

      • Present tense: “I have”, not “I will have”.
      • Positive: “I am debt-free”, not “I am not in debt”.
      • Short: one line, so writing it 18 times a day stays easy.
      • About you: your own feelings and actions, not another person’s choices.
      • Believable: if a big number makes you tense, use the feeling instead (“I feel secure with money”).

      Prefer to picture a whole scene instead of one line? Try scripting manifestation, or our guided Scripting Manifestation workbook.

      Questions

      Is this affirmation generator free?

      Yes. It is free, needs no sign-up, and runs entirely in your browser. Nothing you type is sent or saved anywhere.

      How do I use an affirmation with the 369 method?

      Write the same affirmation 3 times in the morning, 6 times in the afternoon and 9 times at night, every day for 21, 33 or 45 days. The printable sheet on this page helps you keep count.

      What makes a good affirmation?

      It is short, in the present tense as if it is already true, says what you want rather than what you want to avoid, and feels believable when you read it out loud.

      Can I write my own affirmation instead?

      Yes. Type it into the “Write your own” box and the checker shows whether it is present tense, positive and short enough, then you can make a printable sheet from it.

      Do affirmations really work?

      There is no evidence that writing an affirmation makes outside events happen by itself. Used daily, it can keep your goal in mind and help you notice chances and take small steps, which is how we suggest using it.
