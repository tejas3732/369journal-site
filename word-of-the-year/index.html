What is a Word of the Year?

A Word of the Year is a single word you choose to guide the next twelve months. Instead of a long list of resolutions, you keep one word in mind and let it shape small daily choices. “Brave” might mean sending the email you’ve been avoiding. “Rest” might mean saying no to one more plan. It’s easy to remember, so it tends to last longer than a resolution.

Word of the Year ideas

Here are 60 words people often choose, grouped by what they want more of. Read them slowly and notice which one makes you sit up a little.

  • Calm and rest: Peace, Ease, Slow, Rest, Still, Gentle, Simple, Breathe, Soft, Present.
  • Growth and success: Rise, Expand, Build, Focus, Grow, Level up, Bloom, Momentum, Thrive, Achieve.
  • Courage and confidence: Brave, Bold, Worthy, Voice, Fearless, Trust, Begin, Leap, Own, Shine.
  • Love and connection: Open, Together, Tender, Kind, Belong, Connect, Love, Home, Heart, Family.
  • Money and security: Abundance, Steady, Enough, Secure, Wealth, Prosper, Save, Invest, Overflow, Provide.
  • Freedom and joy: Free, Light, Play, Explore, Curious, Savor, Joy, Adventure, Delight, Grateful.

How to choose your word

  1. Look back first. What did this year teach you? What do you not want to repeat?
  2. Name the feeling you want. Think about how you want to feel on 31 December 2027, not just what you want to have.
  3. Find what gets in your way. Your word often answers your biggest obstacle: overthinkers pick “trust”, people-pleasers pick “enough”.
  4. Shortlist three, then sleep on it. The right word usually feels a little uncomfortable and a little exciting.
  5. Test it with one question: “What would [my word] look like today?” If the answer is clear, you’ve found it.

How to use your word every day

  • Put it where you’ll see it. Save your word as your lock screen above, or make a longer affirmation wallpaper.
  • Turn it into an affirmation. “In 2027, I choose to be brave” is something you can write daily. The 369 method is a simple way: write it 3 times in the morning, 6 in the afternoon and 9 at night.
  • Add it to a vision board. Put your word in the middle of a vision board and surround it with pictures of what it looks like.
  • Check in monthly. Ask: where did my word show up this month, and where did I forget it? A journal prompt helps on days you don’t know what to write.
  • Go deeper. Some people pair their word with their 2027 personal year number. If your word is “grateful” or “savor”, our gratitude journal prompts give you something to write each day.
  • Start with a short challenge. Our free 21-day New Year challenge gives you one small task a day to build the habit. For more goals to pick from, see 100 New Year resolution ideas.

Questions

What is a Word of the Year?

It is one word you choose to guide the year ahead, instead of a list of resolutions. You use it to make small daily choices, like asking each morning what your word would look like today.

How do I choose my Word of the Year?

Look back at this year, name how you want to feel at the end of next year, and think about what usually gets in your way. Shortlist three words and pick the one that feels a little exciting and a little uncomfortable. The five-question quiz above does this for you.

Is this Word of the Year generator free?

Yes. There is no sign-up and no email. Your answers and any word you type stay in your browser and are never sent or saved.

Can I use my own word?

Yes. Type it into the Already have a word box and the lock-screen image updates with your word.

What if my word stops fitting halfway through the year?

That's fine. Some people keep the word and look at it from a new angle, others choose a new one in July. The point is to have something that helps you choose, not a rule.

How is a Word of the Year different from a resolution?

A resolution is usually a single outcome, like losing weight, and it is easy to break. A word is a direction. You can't fail at it, you just come back to it.
