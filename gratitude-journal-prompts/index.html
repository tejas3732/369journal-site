        How to start a gratitude journal

        You do not need a special notebook or a long routine. Five minutes a day is plenty. Here is a simple way to begin:

        1. Pick a time you already have. With your morning tea or just before bed works well. Link it to something you do every day.
        2. Choose one prompt from the tool above, or try 3 good things, the classic version where you note three good things from your day.
        3. Be specific. “I’m grateful for my sister” is fine. “I’m grateful my sister called when she saw I was quiet” is better. Details make the feeling real again.
        4. Write why. One extra sentence, “this mattered because…”, turns a list into a thought.
        5. Stop when the time is up. A short habit you keep beats a long one you drop.

        Research on gratitude journaling suggests it can lift mood a little for many people. It is not a cure for anything, but it is a gentle habit that costs nothing.

        What to do on hard days

        Some days gratitude feels fake. That is okay. You do not have to pretend things are fine. On a hard day, try this:

        • Write what is hard, in one honest sentence.
        • Then write one small thing that is still okay: a hot drink, a friend’s message, a bed to sleep in.
        • Let both sentences stand. Gratitude does not cancel pain; it sits next to it.

        The Hard days theme above is written for exactly this. If a prompt feels too much, press New prompt and skip it. If low mood stays for weeks, please talk to a doctor or a counsellor. A journal is support, not treatment.

        Gratitude and manifestation

        Some people write gratitude “in advance”: a thank-you for something they hope for, written as if it is already here. The Gratitude for what’s coming theme does this. To be honest about it, writing like this does not make anything happen by itself. What it can do is shift your attention to what you want and lift your mood, which can make you more likely to take the small steps that lead there. Keep it kind and realistic, and pair it with action.

        Pairing gratitude with the 369 method

        Gratitude prompts by theme

        A few from each theme. The tool above has 108 in all, and you can get a different one every time.

        Everyday small things

        1. What is one small thing from today that made you feel a little lighter?
        2. Which food or drink did you enjoy most today? Describe the first sip or bite.
        3. What did you see today that was quietly beautiful?
        4. What is a sound you like hearing at this time of day?

        People

        1. Who made your day easier this week? What did they do?
        2. Who do you feel safe being yourself with? What do they do that helps?
        3. Write about a person who believed in you before you did.
        4. Who is someone you have never properly thanked? What would you say?

        Yourself

        1. What is one thing you did today that you are proud of, however small?
        2. Which of your strengths helped you this week? How did you use it?
        3. What is something about the way you think or feel that you would not swap?
        4. Write a thank-you note to yourself for something you kept going with.

        Hard days

        1. It is a heavy day. What is one thing, however tiny, that is still okay right now?
        2. What is holding you up today, even if it feels thin: a person, a routine, a cup of tea?
        3. Write what is hard, in one honest sentence. Then write one thing you are still thankful for. Both can be true.
        4. What got you through the last hard time? Can you use a little of it today?

        Body and health

        1. What did your body do for you today that you usually take for granted?
        2. Which part of your body are you thankful for right now, and why?
        3. Write about a time your body healed. What did you learn from it?
        4. What does your body feel like when it is rested? Where did you last feel that?

        Home and places

        1. What is one thing about your home that you are grateful for today?
        2. Describe a corner of your home that feels like yours.
        3. Which place makes you feel calm? Write what you see, hear and smell there.
        4. What is a place you visited that you are glad you saw?

        Work and learning

        1. What is one thing about your work or studies that you are glad of today?
        2. Who at work or school made something easier for you recently?
        3. What did you learn this week that you are glad to know?
        4. Write about a mistake that taught you something useful.

        Seasons and holidays

        1. Thanksgiving: what are three things from this year that you are truly thankful for?
        2. Thanksgiving: who would you like at your table, and what would you thank them for?
        3. What is a food, smell or song that tells you the holidays have arrived?
        4. What are you grateful for in the season you are in right now?

        Gratitude for what's coming

        1. Write one thing you want in your life as if it is already here: “Thank you for…”
        2. Describe your desire in the present tense. What are you thankful for about it?
        3. Write about an ordinary morning after your goal came true. What are you grateful for in it?
        4. Which part of your future are you already thankful for, even before it arrives?

        Questions

        Are these gratitude journal prompts free?

        Yes. They are free, need no sign-up and run in your browser. Prompts you save stay on your device.

        How many gratitude prompts are there?

        There are 108 original prompts across nine themes, from everyday small things to hard days, and a new one every time you press New prompt.

        What is the 3 good things exercise?

        A classic gratitude habit: each day you note three good things and why they happened. Switch to “3 good things” above to get three prompts at once.

        How often should I write in a gratitude journal?

        Daily for five minutes is a good start. Some people prefer two or three times a week. Pick what you will keep doing.

        What if I don't feel grateful?

        Use the Hard days theme. You can write what is hard and one small thing that is still okay. You do not have to force a good feeling.

        Can I print a gratitude journal template?

        Yes. Choose a theme, then print a 30-day journal with one prompt a day and lines to write on, or save it as a PDF.
